# PEDAGOGICAL MASTER PLAN — VERSION 4.1

> **Change note (4.0 → 4.1):** Added Section 5.4 (Readiness Signals) to operationalize how "readiness" is judged, and Section 3.10 (Expression Is Modality-Dependent) to prevent expression modality from being conflated with understanding. Corresponding entries added to Section 25 (Locked Principles) and Section 26 (Source-of-Truth Principle).

### Target Learner

**6-year-old child with a secure Grade 1 baseline, progressing toward advanced cognitive, conceptual and problem-solving capability.**

---

# Executive Summary

This program is designed to develop an **exceptionally strong learner**, not simply an accelerated student.

The objective is to build:

- deep conceptual understanding;

- strong foundational and domain knowledge;

- mathematical and verbal reasoning;

- problem-solving;

- transfer and generalization;

- strategic flexibility;

- creativity;

- metacognition;

- communication;

- productive persistence;

- intellectual independence.

The program is **not defined by completing Grade 2 or Grade 3 content early**.

Content acceleration is permitted where the child demonstrates readiness, but the central objective is **cognitive development**.

The program therefore operates on two independent dimensions:

> **Content progression × Cognitive depth**

A child may demonstrate sophisticated reasoning using relatively simple content, while still developing understanding of more advanced content.

The application provides approximately **15–20 minutes of focused daily learning**, but is only one component of the child's broader education. Reading, conversation, play, physical activity, experimentation, exploration, building, drawing and family interaction remain essential.

The Master Plan is the source of truth for all subsequent curriculum, assessment, adaptive-learning and product decisions.

---

# 1. EDUCATIONAL NORTH STAR

The program seeks to develop a learner who increasingly:

> **knows, understands, reasons, transfers, creates, communicates and learns independently.**

An advanced learner is not simply one who knows more content than peers.

She increasingly:

1. understands underlying ideas rather than memorizing procedures;

2. connects new knowledge to existing knowledge;

3. recognizes patterns and relationships;

4. solves unfamiliar problems;

5. chooses and compares strategies;

6. recognizes when a familiar idea applies in an unfamiliar situation;

7. explains or represents her thinking;

8. identifies and learns from mistakes;

9. changes strategy when an approach is ineffective;

10. creates explanations, questions, problems and solutions;

11. persists through productive struggle;

12. knows when to continue independently and when to seek help.

The ultimate objective is:

> **Strong knowledge + deep understanding + flexible reasoning + increasing intellectual independence.**

---

# 2. END-OF-PROGRAM CAPABILITY PROFILE

The end state is defined through **capabilities**, not completion of a particular grade syllabus.

The child should demonstrate a combination of:

- secure foundational knowledge;

- selected advanced content where appropriate;

- flexible reasoning;

- transfer;

- creativity;

- strategic flexibility;

- communication;

- metacognition;

- productive persistence;

- increasing independence.

Development is expected to remain uneven across domains.

The child does **not** need to be uniformly advanced in every subject.

---

## 2.1 Mathematics

A successful learner can:

- reason confidently about number, quantity, operations, relationships, patterns, measurement, geometry and data;

- use secure foundations to solve unfamiliar and non-routine problems;

- represent mathematical ideas in multiple ways;

- understand equality and relationships rather than viewing mathematics only as calculation;

- choose appropriate strategies and explain or demonstrate why they work;

- find more than one solution when appropriate;

- recognize inefficient or inappropriate approaches;

- identify mathematical structures across superficially different problems;

- use selected advanced concepts when prerequisites are secure;

- generalize patterns and relationships using age-appropriate representations;

- create mathematical problems, patterns or models.

The end-state is **not defined by completing a Grade 3 mathematics syllabus**.

---

## 2.2 English Language, Reading & Communication

A successful learner can:

- read accurately and with increasing fluency;

- understand literal meaning;

- make reasonable inferences;

- connect ideas across a text;

- use vocabulary from context and prior knowledge;

- recognize useful word structures;

- understand increasingly complex sentence and text structures;

- distinguish evidence from interpretation where appropriate;

- explain ideas clearly;

- construct coherent written and spoken responses;

- organize narratives, explanations and opinions;

- revise and improve communication;

- use evidence from text to support interpretation;

- generate questions and ideas from reading;

- communicate thinking through words, writing, drawings, diagrams and other representations.

The objective is not simply early reading fluency.

It is:

> **language as a tool for thinking, understanding and communicating.**

---

## 2.3 Science & EVS

A successful learner can:

- possess meaningful knowledge about the natural and physical world;

- observe carefully;

- classify according to meaningful properties;

- recognize patterns;

- make reasonable predictions;

- distinguish observations from interpretations;

- use evidence to support or revise explanations;

- reason about simple causal relationships;

- construct age-appropriate models of systems;

- compare explanations;

- investigate questions through observation and simple inquiry;

- connect scientific ideas to everyday experiences;

- ask increasingly sophisticated questions about how and why things work.

The objective is:

> **Curiosity + knowledge + observation + evidence + explanation + causal reasoning.**

---

## 2.4 Logical Reasoning

A successful learner can:

- recognize patterns and relationships;

- classify using relevant attributes;

- reason by analogy;

- sequence information;

- reason spatially;

- work with multiple constraints;

- reason about conditional relationships;

- identify contradictions or inconsistencies;

- detect errors in reasoning;

- distinguish relevant from irrelevant information;

- reason from evidence to conclusions;

- revise conclusions when information changes;

- solve unfamiliar reasoning problems without relying solely on memorized puzzle formats.

The target is:

> **Understanding and manipulating relationships, constraints and evidence.**

---

## 2.5 World Knowledge & Understanding

A successful learner can:

- possess meaningful knowledge about geography, history, culture, nature, science, technology and human systems;

- organize facts into connected concepts;

- explain relationships between people, places, resources, systems and events;

- understand simple cause-and-effect relationships;

- compare different places, times, systems and ways of living;

- use knowledge to explain everyday phenomena;

- recognize that systems interact;

- ask questions extending beyond isolated facts;

- use knowledge from one context to make sense of another.

The objective is:

> **A rich, organized mental model of the world rather than disconnected trivia.**

---

## 2.6 Metacognition

A successful learner increasingly can:

- describe or demonstrate how she approached a problem;

- recognize when she understands something and when she does not;

- identify when she is stuck;

- select a reasonable next step;

- check whether an answer makes sense;

- identify mistakes and learn from them;

- compare strategies;

- recognize which strategy was useful and why;

- reflect on what changed her understanding;

- ask for appropriate help.

Metacognition is an authentic learning capability, not a separate subject.

---

## 2.7 Strategic Flexibility

A successful learner increasingly can:

- choose among strategies;

- compare strategies;

- recognize inefficient strategies;

- abandon ineffective approaches;

- try another representation;

- approach a problem differently;

- recognize when a familiar method does not fit;

- identify common structure across different problem types.

The objective is not to force multiple methods.

It is:

> **Knowing when a strategy is appropriate, when it is not, and what to try next.**

---

## 2.8 Creativity

A successful learner increasingly can:

- generate multiple ideas;

- ask her own questions;

- create problems;

- invent explanations;

- design simple investigations;

- construct alternative solutions;

- modify existing ideas;

- imagine alternative outcomes;

- create stories, models, puzzles or representations;

- combine ideas from different domains.

Creativity is a mode of learning and production, not a separate academic subject.

---

## 2.9 Communication

The child should increasingly make her thinking visible through:

- speech;

- writing;

- drawings;

- diagrams;

- physical models;

- symbols;

- demonstrations;

- examples;

- comparisons of strategies.

Sophisticated verbal explanation is not required in every situation.

The objective is:

> **Increasing ability to communicate thinking in appropriate forms.**

See Section 3.10 for how the modality of expression affects how this capability should be assessed at this age.

---

## 2.10 Productive Persistence

A successful learner increasingly:

- tolerates temporary uncertainty;

- remains engaged when an answer is not immediately available;

- experiments with alternatives;

- recovers from mistakes;

- distinguishes productive struggle from missing prerequisites;

- seeks help appropriately;

- returns to difficult ideas;

- experiences curiosity rather than fear around difficult problems.

The objective is:

> **"I know what to do when I get stuck."**

# 3. DEVELOPMENTAL PRINCIPLES

## 3.1 Age-Appropriate Does Not Mean Grade-Restricted

A six-year-old may encounter ideas normally associated with older children when those ideas can be represented through:

- concrete experience;

- visual models;

- intuitive relationships;

- examples;

- stories;

- experimentation;

- appropriate scaffolding.

The primary constraints are:

- conceptual appropriateness;

- cognitive load;

- language demands;

- prerequisite knowledge;

- developmental readiness.

The question is:

> **Does this advance development meaningfully?**

Not:

> "What grade is this topic?"

---

## 3.2 Cognitive Load

Tasks should challenge the intended capability without unnecessary load from:

- excessive instructions;

- unnecessary language;

- irrelevant information;

- multiple simultaneous novel demands;

- unsupported abstraction.

The objective is to challenge **thinking**, not simply increase difficulty.

---

## 3.3 Productive Struggle

### Harmful Difficulty

The child lacks a prerequisite, cannot access the task, or is experiencing excessive cognitive load.

### Productive Struggle

The child has the necessary foundations but needs time, experimentation, persistence or a change of strategy.

The program must protect productive struggle rather than scaffold every difficulty away.

---

## 3.4 Curiosity and Autonomy

Learning should encourage:

- questioning;

- exploration;

- choice;

- experimentation;

- explanation;

- discovery.

The child should increasingly experience herself as:

> **Someone who can figure things out.**

---

## 3.5 Knowledge Matters

Reasoning requires knowledge.

The program therefore rejects the false choice between:

> knowledge versus thinking.

Instead:

> **Knowledge provides the material on which thinking operates.**

---

## 3.6 Representation Matters

Concepts should, where appropriate, be experienced across:

> **Concrete ↔ Visual ↔ Verbal ↔ Symbolic ↔ Abstract**

The child should understand relationships among representations rather than becoming dependent on one format.

---

## 3.7 Retention Matters

Important learning should become durable and remain accessible:

- after time has passed;

- after intervening learning;

- in changed contexts;

- without immediate prompting.

---

## 3.8 Misconceptions Differ from Forgetting

Difficulty may arise because the child:

1. has never learned the concept;

2. has partially learned it;

3. has forgotten it;

4. holds a misconception;

5. understands it but cannot yet transfer it.

These are different pedagogical conditions and should not be treated as equivalent.

---

## 3.9 Executive Function Develops Authentically

Executive function should develop through meaningful learning demands such as:

- planning;

- holding constraints in mind;

- monitoring progress;

- switching strategies;

- delaying an impulsive answer;

- remembering a goal;

- checking work.

Artificial "brain training" is not a program objective.

---

## 3.10 Expression Is Modality-Dependent

The capability to "construct," "explain," or "write" a response (Sections 2.2, 2.9, 11.2) depends on the **modality** through which the child produces it — for example typing, dictation, tapping/selecting, drawing, or independent handwriting — and not on understanding alone.

At six years old, these modalities are not interchangeable. Handwriting and typing speed, and the ability to read instructions independently, vary widely and are still developing. A child may understand and be able to explain an idea aloud well before she can produce an equivalent written response unaided.

Therefore:

> **Written or verbal expression should be assessed relative to the modality used, not treated as a single undifferentiated capability.**

Downstream design must specify, for each context, which modality is assumed (e.g., typed response, dictated response, tap-to-select, drawn representation), and must not infer a deficit in understanding from a limitation of the expression modality itself.

# 4. DUAL-AXIS LEARNING MODEL

## Axis 1 — Content Progression

> **Foundational → Core → Extended → Advanced**

This describes content complexity and breadth.

Grade labels may be used as reference points but are not the architecture.

## Axis 2 — Cognitive Depth

> **Understand → Apply → Reason → Generalize → Create**

These describe forms of cognitive engagement.

They are **not**:

- developmental age levels;

- IQ categories;

- achievement ranks;

- mandatory stages.

A child may:

- create with foundational content;

- generalize simple mathematics;

- reason deeply about familiar science;

- understand advanced content without yet transferring it.

---

## 4.1 Independence of the Axes

For example:

> **Grade 2-level content + Generalize-level thinking**

may represent stronger learning than:

> **Grade 3-level content + Recall-level performance.**

Likewise:

> **Grade 1 content + Create-level thinking**

can represent sophisticated cognitive development.

Content level must therefore never be used as a proxy for cognitive development.

---

# 5. PREREQUISITE DEPENDENCY NETWORK

The curriculum should not operate as a simple:

> Grade 1 → Grade 2 → Grade 3

ladder.

Each important concept should eventually be represented through its dependencies.

For each target concept, distinguish:

### Required Prerequisites

Necessary for meaningful access.

### Strongly Supportive Prerequisites

Substantially improve readiness but may not be strictly necessary.

### Useful Background Knowledge

Helpful but should not automatically block progression.

This distinction prevents unrelated gaps from becoming artificial barriers.

---

## 5.1 Readiness-Driven Acceleration

Acceleration is appropriate when:

- required prerequisites are sufficiently secure;

- the child can meaningfully access the concept;

- cognitive demands are appropriate;

- advancement is likely to deepen learning.

Acceleration is not dependent on:

- completing every earlier-grade topic;

- reaching Generalize across all previous content;

- maximizing speed;

- proving that the child is ahead.

---

## 5.2 When the Child Is Not Ready

When a target concept is inaccessible:

1. identify the relevant prerequisite;

2. strengthen it;

3. use appropriate representations;

4. revisit the target when readiness improves.

Do not automatically respond with repetitive practice of the target concept.

---

## 5.3 When the Child Is Ready Early

When readiness is demonstrated:

- deepen the current concept;

- increase cognitive complexity;

- explore transfer;

- explore related concepts;

- introduce selected advanced content;

- encourage creation and generalization.

Acceleration should be selective, not global.

---

## 5.4 Readiness Signals

Readiness (Sections 5.1–5.3, Phase E) is a **holistic judgment inferred from multiple converging signals**, not a single score, test result or formula.

Relevant signals include:

### Security

Required prerequisites are demonstrated at or above the **Secure** level (Section 7).

### Independence

The prerequisite is used with minimal scaffolding, not only under heavy support.

### Spontaneous Transfer

The child applies a relevant idea unprompted, outside the moment or context in which it was taught.

### Consistency

The signal holds across more than one occasion, not a single instance.

### Engagement

The child approaches the material with interest and genuine attempt, rather than guessing or avoiding it.

No single signal is sufficient on its own. High accuracy without independence, or one strong session without consistency, should not by itself establish readiness. Absence of one signal should prompt closer observation rather than an automatic pass/fail determination.

This section constrains, but deliberately does not fully operationalize, readiness. Exact thresholds, signal weighting and detection logic remain downstream Assessment / Adaptive Learning Framework decisions, and must be **derived from this principle rather than substitute for it**.

---

# 6. DEFINITION OF ADVANCED DEVELOPMENT

Advanced development integrates four dimensions:

### 1. Knowledge

Meaningful, accurate and increasingly connected domain knowledge.

### 2. Conceptual Understanding

Understanding underlying relationships, mechanisms and structures.

### 3. Reasoning & Transfer

Applying knowledge flexibly, recognizing structure across contexts and solving unfamiliar problems.

### 4. Intellectual Independence

Selecting strategies, monitoring thinking, adapting when stuck and seeking help appropriately.

These are integrated dimensions, not numerical scores to be added together.

---

# 7. MASTERY PHILOSOPHY

Mastery is a **holistic pedagogical judgment**, not a universal score.

A useful conceptual progression is:

> **Introduced → Developing → Secure → Flexible → Generalized / Durable**

These are not rigid universal stages.

---

## Introduced

The child has encountered the concept and can participate with appropriate support.

## Developing

The child demonstrates partial understanding or inconsistent independent use.

## Secure

The child can use the concept independently and reliably in expected contexts.

## Flexible

The child can use the concept across different representations, contexts or structures.

## Generalized / Durable

The child demonstrates meaningful transfer, recognizes underlying principles in unfamiliar situations and retains important learning over time.

---

## 7.1 Mastery Is Skill-Specific

Evidence differs by skill.

Examples:

- mathematical procedures may require accuracy, fluency and strategy selection;

- mathematical concepts may require representation, explanation and transfer;

- reading comprehension may require inference and evidence;

- scientific understanding may require observation, evidence and explanation;

- creativity may require generation, modification and originality appropriate to age.

No single evidence requirement applies universally.

---

# 8. TRANSFER & GENERALIZATION

Transfer is a primary indicator of genuine understanding.

It is not equivalent to increasing difficulty.

### Level 1 — Surface Transfer

Same structure with changed details.

### Level 2 — Contextual Transfer

Same principle in a different context.

### Level 3 — Representational Transfer

Same concept in a different representation.

### Level 4 — Structural Transfer

Different surface structure while preserving the underlying principle.

### Level 5 — Novel Transfer

The child independently recognizes and applies an underlying principle in a genuinely unfamiliar situation.

The target capability is:

> **"This looks different, but I recognize what kind of thinking is needed."**

Transfer is developed throughout the curriculum, not reserved for advanced stages.

---

# 9. DURABLE LEARNING: CONSOLIDATION, RETRIEVAL & INTERLEAVING

Learning should progress from:

> **Understanding → Consolidation → Retrieval → Appropriate Interleaving → Transfer**

These processes serve different purposes.

### Practice

Builds understanding and fluency.

### Retrieval

Strengthens access to previously learned knowledge.

### Transfer

Tests whether knowledge can be recognized and used when the surface situation changes.

A child may therefore:

- perform well in practice;

- struggle with retrieval;

- retrieve successfully;

- yet still struggle to transfer.

These are different learning conditions.

Initial learning should provide sufficient focused experience to establish understanding.

Later learning should increasingly require:

- retrieval;

- strategy discrimination;

- changed contexts;

- transfer.

Interleaving and transfer should not be introduced so aggressively that they interfere with initial concept formation.

Exact spacing and scheduling belong to the later Assessment / Adaptive Learning Framework.

# 12. DEVELOPMENTAL PROGRESSION

Progression is capability-driven rather than grade-driven.

## Phase A — Foundation

Establish essential knowledge and identify gaps.

## Phase B — Consolidation

Strengthen understanding, independence, retrieval and representation.

## Phase C — Extension

Introduce new concepts when prerequisites permit.

## Phase D — Deepening

Increase reasoning, transfer, strategy selection and explanation.

## Phase E — Selective Acceleration

Introduce advanced concepts where readiness is demonstrated.

## Phase F — Generalization & Independence

Increase transfer, creation, cross-domain reasoning, strategic flexibility and self-directed learning.

These phases are **not rigid chronological stages**.

The child may simultaneously be:

- consolidating one concept;

- extending another;

- reasoning deeply about another;

- exploring advanced material elsewhere.

---

# 13. BREADTH, DEPTH & PRIORITIZATION

Every capability should eventually be classified as:

### Essential Foundations

Required for robust future learning.

### Core Development

Expected to receive meaningful development.

### Enrichment / Advanced

Pursued when readiness, interest and opportunity justify it.

The program should prioritize **depth over superficial breadth**, while maintaining sufficient breadth of knowledge to support curiosity and transfer.

The objective is not to make the child "advanced at everything."

---

# 14. CONTENT ACCELERATION VS COGNITIVE ACCELERATION

### Content Acceleration

Learning concepts typically encountered at an older age or grade.

### Cognitive Acceleration

Increasing ability to:

- reason;

- generalize;

- transfer;

- create;

- select strategies;

- monitor thinking;

- solve unfamiliar problems;

- work independently.

Cognitive acceleration can occur without content acceleration.

Content acceleration should occur only when it meaningfully contributes to development.

The program must never use advanced content merely to make the learner appear advanced.

---

# 15. CAPABILITY-DRIVEN DOMAIN BALANCE

The five domains are **not intended to receive equal time simply for symmetry**.

### Mathematics and English

Require frequent longitudinal development because they have extensive foundational and prerequisite structures.

### Science / EVS and World Knowledge

Require regular exposure to build connected knowledge and inquiry.

### Logical Reasoning

Has an explicit developmental strand **and** should be embedded deliberately across Mathematics, English, Science and World Knowledge.

### Cross-Cutting Capabilities

Metacognition, creativity, strategic flexibility, communication, persistence and executive function should primarily be developed through authentic work across domains.

Therefore:

> **Time allocation should reflect developmental importance, prerequisite structure, learning needs, readiness and opportunities for meaningful integration.**

Balance should be reviewed over meaningful periods rather than optimized session by session.

Equal time is not the objective.

Balanced development is.

---

# 16. LOGICAL REASONING — LANGUAGE REQUIREMENT

A specific locked principle applies to Logic.

> **Construct-Irrelevant Language Principle:**  
> When a task is intended primarily to develop or evidence logical reasoning, linguistic complexity should be controlled so that unnecessary reading or language-processing demands do not obscure the reasoning capability being developed.

Language complexity may intentionally increase when language comprehension is itself part of the learning objective.

Such tasks should then be treated as:

> **Integrated language-and-reasoning experiences**

rather than pure measures of logical reasoning.

The objective is not to make logic language-free.

It is to prevent irrelevant language difficulty from becoming an accidental confound.

---

# 17. VISUAL-TO-SYMBOLIC DEVELOPMENT

The child should move progressively among:

> **Concrete ↔ Visual ↔ Verbal ↔ Symbolic ↔ Abstract**

This is **not a rigid one-way progression**.

Symbolic representations should be introduced when they provide a useful way to represent relationships the child already has sufficient conceptual access to.

Concrete and visual representations should remain available whenever they improve understanding.

Therefore:

> **Symbolic fluency does not establish conceptual mastery.**

Likewise:

> **Continued use of concrete or visual representations does not indicate weak understanding if they support sophisticated reasoning.**

Increasing independence with symbolic and abstract reasoning is an outcome of conceptual development, not a measure of advancement by itself.

# 20. SUPPORT & PRODUCTIVE STRUGGLE

Support should match the nature of the difficulty.

### Missing prerequisite

> Strengthen the prerequisite.

### Productive struggle

> Allow thinking, experimentation and time.

### Ineffective strategy

> Encourage reflection or strategy change.

### Excessive cognitive load

> Reduce irrelevant demand.

The objective is not maximum independence at every moment.

It is:

> **Increasingly appropriate independence.**

# 21. CREATIVITY & CREATION

The child should periodically move from:

> **Answering → Explaining → Modifying → Generating → Creating**

Examples:

### Mathematics

Create a problem with a specified solution.

### English

Create an alternative story ending.

### Science

Design a simple investigation or explanatory model.

### Logic

Create a puzzle or constraint system.

### World Knowledge

Build or draw a model explaining how a system works.

Creation is a genuine learning outcome.

# 25. PEDAGOGICAL PRINCIPLES NOW LOCKED

The following decisions are now considered **closed architectural principles** and should not be reconsidered in downstream documents without an explicit decision to revise the Master Plan.

## 25.1 Logic and Language

Unnecessary linguistic complexity must not confound primarily logical reasoning tasks.

## 25.2 Representation and Abstraction

Concrete, visual, verbal, symbolic and abstract representations should be used flexibly rather than as a rigid developmental ladder.

## 25.3 Durable Learning

Understanding precedes meaningful consolidation; later learning incorporates retrieval, appropriate interleaving and transfer.

## 25.4 Domain Balance

Time allocation is capability-driven rather than equally distributed.

Mathematics and literacy require frequent longitudinal attention.

Science and World Knowledge build connected knowledge through regular exposure.

Logic has both an explicit strand and a cross-domain role.

Metacognition, creativity, strategic flexibility, communication, persistence and executive function are primarily cross-cutting.

## 25.5 Content and Cognitive Depth

Content progression and cognitive depth remain independent axes.

## 25.6 Prerequisite-Driven Acceleration

Advanced content is introduced based on readiness and prerequisite structure, not grade labels.

## 25.7 Productive Struggle

The system should preserve productive difficulty while responding appropriately to missing prerequisites and excessive cognitive load.

## 25.8 Transfer

Transfer is a primary indicator of genuine understanding and develops progressively from surface variation to novel application.

## 25.9 Mastery

Mastery is holistic and skill-specific rather than a universal numerical threshold.

## 25.10 Real-World Learning

The application complements rather than replaces broader childhood learning experiences.

## 25.11 Readiness

Readiness is inferred holistically from converging signals — security, independence, spontaneous transfer, consistency and engagement — never from a single signal, score or formula (Section 5.4).

## 25.12 Expression Modality

Written and verbal expression capabilities must be assessed relative to the modality used to produce them (typed, dictated, tapped, drawn, handwritten), and must never have a modality limitation mistaken for a limitation of understanding (Section 3.10).

# 28. FINAL ARCHITECTURAL TEST

The Master Plan now answers:

### 1. What kind of learner are we developing?

A knowledgeable, conceptually grounded, flexible, curious, creative and increasingly independent thinker.

### 2. What should she know?

Secure foundations plus meaningful connected domain knowledge and selected advanced content where appropriate.

### 3. What should she understand?

Underlying concepts, relationships, mechanisms and structures.

### 4. What should she reason about?

Patterns, relationships, evidence, constraints, causes, systems and unfamiliar problems.

### 5. What should she solve?

Both familiar and non-routine problems requiring appropriate strategy selection and adaptation.

### 6. What should she transfer?

Underlying principles across changed numbers, contexts, representations, structures and genuinely novel situations.

### 7. What should she create?

Questions, problems, explanations, models, stories, investigations, puzzles and alternative solutions.

### 8. What should she understand about her own thinking?

How she approached problems, what worked, what failed, what she knows, where she is stuck and what she can try next.

### 9. What is essential versus enrichment?

Foundations are prioritized; core capabilities receive sustained development; advanced capabilities are pursued selectively according to readiness and interest.

### 10. What are the prerequisites?

Each important capability is ultimately mapped through required, supportive and background dependencies.

### 11. What is the developmental sequence?

Foundation → Consolidation → Extension → Deepening → Selective Acceleration → Generalization & Independence.

### 12. What does advanced mean?

Integration of knowledge, conceptual understanding, reasoning/transfer and intellectual independence.

### 13. How does content acceleration differ from cognitive acceleration?

Content acceleration concerns what the child learns; cognitive acceleration concerns how deeply, flexibly and independently she thinks.

### 14. What happens when she is ready?

Deepen, generalize, extend and selectively accelerate.

### 15. What happens when she is not ready?

Identify and strengthen the relevant prerequisite rather than forcing the advanced content.

### 16. What belongs inside versus outside the application?

The application provides focused learning; reading, play, exploration, conversation, physical activity and real-world experiences remain essential outside it.

### 17. What should she look like at the end?

A strong, curious, flexible, knowledgeable, persistent and increasingly independent learner.

### 18. What should the program deliberately NOT optimize for?

Speed, volume, grade labels, constant correctness, premature acceleration, excessive screen time, artificial difficulty, rote memorization and competitive performance metrics.

**All eighteen questions now have explicit answers within the architecture.**

# 29. FINAL DEFINITION

The program is:

> **A capability-driven learning system in which content progresses according to prerequisites and readiness, while cognitive depth develops independently through understanding, application, reasoning, generalization and creation.**

Its purpose is not to make a six-year-old complete an older child's syllabus.

Its purpose is to help her become:

> **a knowledgeable, conceptually grounded, curious, flexible, creative, persistent and increasingly independent thinker.**

Content acceleration is permitted when it serves that objective.

Cognitive acceleration is expected throughout the program.

Transfer is a core indicator of understanding.

Mastery is multidimensional and skill-specific.

Prerequisites govern readiness.

Productive struggle is protected.

Creativity and metacognition are integrated across domains.

Real-world learning remains essential.

And the child's development is never reduced to a single grade level, score or measure of speed.

## This is the pedagogical destination and the educational path.

All subsequent curriculum, assessment, adaptive-learning and product decisions must serve this architecture rather than redefine it.
