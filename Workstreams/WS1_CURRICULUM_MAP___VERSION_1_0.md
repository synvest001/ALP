# 1. Purpose and Governing Principles

This Curriculum Map translates the Pedagogical Master Plan into a structured:

> **Domain → Strand → Skill → Subskill → Prerequisites → Developmental Progression**

architecture.

It is the first operational representation of the curriculum described in the Master Plan.

The map must preserve the following architectural principles:

1. **Content progression and cognitive depth remain independent.**
2. **Prerequisites govern readiness rather than grade labels.**
3. **Development is capability-driven rather than grade-driven.**
4. **Essential Foundations receive priority because they support future learning.**
5. **Core Development receives sustained longitudinal attention.**
6. **Enrichment / Advanced content is pursued selectively according to readiness, interest and opportunity.**
7. **Phases A–F are developmental orientations, not chronological gates.**
8. **Logical reasoning exists both as an explicit domain and as a cross-domain capability.**
9. **Metacognition, creativity, strategic flexibility, communication, persistence and executive function are primarily developed through authentic domain work rather than as isolated subjects.**
10. **The Curriculum Map describes skills and dependencies; cognitive-depth and transfer classifications belong to tasks and therefore to the Question Specification layer.**

These principles are inherited from the Master Plan and must not be redefined locally.

# 2. Curriculum Architecture

The first draft contains five primary curriculum domains:

1. Mathematics & Mathematical Thinking
2. English Language, Reading & Reasoning
3. Science / EVS & Scientific Inquiry
4. Logical Reasoning
5. World Knowledge & Understanding

Cross-cutting capabilities are represented separately because the Master Plan explicitly states that they should primarily develop through authentic work across domains:

- Metacognition
- Strategic Flexibility
- Productive Persistence
- Executive Function
- Representational Flexibility
- Communication
- Creativity
- Misconception Sensitivity
- Retention & Durability

These are **not currently treated as independent academic subjects**.

# 3. Developmental Progression Framework

The curriculum uses the Master Plan's six developmental phases.

| Phase                                 | Primary purpose                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Foundation**                    | Establish essential knowledge and identify gaps                                                       |
| **B — Consolidation**                 | Strengthen understanding, independence, retrieval and representation                                  |
| **C — Extension**                     | Introduce new concepts when prerequisites permit                                                      |
| **D — Deepening**                     | Increase reasoning, transfer, strategy selection and explanation                                      |
| **E — Selective Acceleration**        | Introduce advanced concepts where readiness is demonstrated                                           |
| **F — Generalization & Independence** | Increase transfer, creation, cross-domain reasoning, strategic flexibility and self-directed learning |

These phases are **not chronological stages**.

A learner may simultaneously be in different phases across different skills. For example, a child may be consolidating one mathematical skill while extending another and reasoning deeply about a science concept.

The phase associated with a skill therefore represents its **normal developmental emphasis**, not a gate that must be completed before other learning can occur.

---

# 4. Priority Classification

Every subskill should eventually receive one of three curriculum-priority classifications.

### Essential Foundation

Required for robust future learning.

### Core Development

Expected to receive meaningful and sustained development.

### Enrichment / Advanced

Pursued when readiness, interest and opportunity justify it.

The classification does not imply that enrichment skills are unimportant. It establishes the intended priority of curriculum investment.

---

# 5. Prerequisite Model

For every subskill, prerequisites will eventually be classified as:

### Required

Necessary for meaningful access.

### Strongly Supportive

Substantially improves readiness but may not be strictly necessary.

### Useful Background

Helpful context that should not automatically block progression.

The Curriculum Map should never turn the curriculum into a simple:

> Grade 1 → Grade 2 → Grade 3

sequence.

Where the Master Plan identifies a relationship but does not provide sufficient detail to specify the exact prerequisite, the relationship is marked:

> **TBD — requires curriculum-level dependency analysis**

This prevents the Curriculum Map from silently inventing pedagogical decisions that belong in later validation.

# 9. Domain 4 — Logical Reasoning

Logical reasoning has an explicit curriculum strand and must also be embedded across Mathematics, English, Science and World Knowledge.

A critical Master Plan constraint applies:

> When a task primarily measures logical reasoning, unnecessary linguistic complexity must not obscure the reasoning capability being assessed.

The Curriculum Map itself does not classify individual tasks as pure reasoning or integrated language-and-reasoning; that distinction belongs downstream to Question Specification.

# 11. Cross-Cutting Capabilities

These capabilities are deliberately **not separate subjects** in the first draft.

They should be developed and evidenced through authentic work across the five domains.

---

## 11.1 Strategic Flexibility

Capabilities:

- choosing among strategies;
- comparing strategies;
- recognizing inefficient strategies;
- abandoning ineffective approaches;
- trying another representation;
- approaching a problem differently;
- recognizing when a familiar method does not fit;
- identifying common structure across problem types.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Phase B → Phase C → Phase D → Phase E → Phase F

Primary domain integration:

- Mathematics
- Logic
- Science
- English reasoning
- World Knowledge

---

## 11.2 Metacognition

Capabilities:

- describing or demonstrating an approach;
- recognizing understanding versus uncertainty;
- identifying when stuck;
- selecting a reasonable next step;
- checking whether an answer makes sense;
- identifying mistakes;
- comparing strategies;
- recognizing what worked and why;
- reflecting on changed understanding;
- asking for appropriate help.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Phase B → Phase C → Phase D → Phase E → Phase F

---

## 11.3 Productive Persistence

Capabilities:

- tolerating temporary uncertainty;
- remaining engaged;
- experimenting with alternatives;
- recovering from mistakes;
- distinguishing productive struggle from missing prerequisites;
- seeking help appropriately;
- returning to difficult ideas;
- maintaining curiosity around difficulty.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Phase A → Phase B → Phase C → Phase D → Phase F

This capability should not be represented as "difficulty tolerance" alone.

The objective is:

> **"I know what to do when I get stuck."**

---

## 11.4 Executive Function

Capabilities developed through meaningful learning demands:

- planning;
- holding constraints in mind;
- monitoring progress;
- switching strategies;
- delaying an impulsive answer;
- remembering a goal;
- checking work.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Across all phases

No artificial "brain training" strand is created.

---

## 11.5 Representational Flexibility

Capabilities:

- moving between concrete and visual representations;
- moving between visual and verbal representations;
- using symbolic representations where appropriate;
- connecting representations;
- using concrete/visual representations when they improve understanding;
- increasing independence with symbolic and abstract reasoning when conceptually ready.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Phase A → Phase B → Phase C → Phase D → Phase E/F

Important constraint:

> Symbolic fluency does not establish conceptual mastery.

---

## 11.6 Communication

Capabilities:

- speech;
- writing;
- drawing;
- diagrams;
- physical models;
- symbols;
- demonstrations;
- examples;
- comparisons of strategies.

Normal developmental emphasis:

> Across all phases

Expression must remain modality-dependent.

The system must not interpret difficulty with handwriting, typing or another output modality as evidence of weak conceptual understanding.

---

## 11.7 Creativity

Capabilities:

- generating multiple ideas;
- asking questions;
- creating problems;
- inventing explanations;
- designing simple investigations;
- constructing alternative solutions;
- modifying ideas;
- imagining alternative outcomes;
- creating stories, models, puzzles or representations;
- combining ideas from different domains.

Developmental progression:

> **Answering → Explaining → Modifying → Generating → Creating**

This progression should be represented across domains rather than as a separate creativity curriculum.

---

## 11.8 Misconception Sensitivity

The curriculum must distinguish between:

1. never learned;
2. partially learned;
3. forgotten;
4. misconception;
5. understood but not yet transferable.

This distinction is primarily important downstream for Assessment and Adaptive Learning, but the Curriculum Map should preserve it as a property of the learning architecture.

---

## 11.9 Retention & Durability

Important skills should eventually support:

- retrieval after time;
- retrieval after intervening learning;
- application in changed contexts;
- access without immediate prompting.

Retention is therefore a developmental property of important skills rather than a separate curriculum subject.

# 13. Domain Priority Architecture

The Master Plan explicitly rejects equal time allocation simply for symmetry.

### Mathematics

Frequent longitudinal development because of its extensive prerequisite structure.

### English

Frequent longitudinal development because of foundational language, reading and communication dependencies.

### Science / EVS

Regular exposure to build connected knowledge and inquiry.

### World Knowledge

Regular exposure to build a connected mental model of the world.

### Logical Reasoning

Both:

- an explicit developmental strand;
- deliberate integration across other domains.

### Cross-Cutting Capabilities

Primarily developed through authentic work rather than isolated instructional blocks.

Therefore the eventual adaptive system should optimize for:

> **balanced development over meaningful periods**

rather than equal domain time in every session.

# 15. What Is Explicitly NOT in This Curriculum Map

The following are intentionally excluded from this artifact:

### Cognitive-depth tags

The Curriculum Map does not assign:

> Understand / Apply / Reason / Generalize / Create

to skills.

These are properties of tasks and belong to the Question Specification layer.

### Transfer-level tags

The Curriculum Map does not assign:

> Level 1 → Level 5

to skills.

Transfer is evidenced through tasks and therefore belongs downstream.

### Mastery thresholds

No percentages, accuracy thresholds or universal numeric criteria are specified.

### Response modality

The map identifies expression capabilities but does not prescribe whether a particular task uses:

- typing;
- dictation;
- tapping;
- drawing;
- handwriting.

That belongs downstream.

### Adaptive algorithms

No selection or sequencing algorithm is defined here.

### Retrieval intervals

No exact spacing or scheduling is defined here.

### Question formats

No item-level formats are defined here.

### Product / UX

No screen, interaction or interface decisions are made here.

This separation follows the Implementation Plan and prevents the Curriculum Map from becoming an accidental replacement for the Assessment, Adaptive Learning or Question Specification layers.

## 16.10 Master Plan Compliance

# 20. Current Architectural Position

The Curriculum Map is now positioned as:

> **The structured content and dependency layer between the Pedagogical Master Plan and the Assessment / Mastery Framework.**

Its job is to answer:

> **What capabilities and subskills are we developing, how are they related, what is foundational versus core versus advanced, and what normally precedes what?**

It deliberately does **not** answer:

> How do we assess mastery?

> How does the adaptive engine select the next task?

> How difficult is a particular question?

> What transfer level does a question represent?

> What response modality should a particular item use?
